\subsection*{Assignment 3}

Given: $M = \{x \in \Re^n | f(x)=0\}$ and\\
$\gamma : \Re \rightarrow M$, with $\gamma(0) = x$ and $\gamma'(0) = v$\\
\\
To show: $\gradient f(x)$ is orthogonal to $v$, or $\gradient f(x)\cdot v = 0$\\
\\
Let $h(t) = f(\gamma(t))$, then $\forall t: h(t) = 0$, because $\forall t: \gamma(t) \in M$\\
Furthermore $\forall t: h'(t) = 0$, because $h(t)$ is constant as shown above.\\
Additionally $h'(t) = \gradient f(\gamma(t)) \cdot \gamma'(t)$, due to chain rule.\\
Now let $t=0 \implies h'(0) = 0 = \gradient f(\underbrace{\gamma(0)}_{x}) \cdot \underbrace{\gamma'(0)}_{v}$\\
$\implies 0 = \gradient f(x)\cdot v$ $\square$
